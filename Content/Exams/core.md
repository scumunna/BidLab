:::q Campaign Briefs
question: A new campaign brief lands on your desk. Which element must be settled before media planning can responsibly begin?
- A finalized creative concept
- A clear business objective
- A preferred vendor list
- A reporting cadence
answer: 1
explain: The business objective anchors every later decision (strategy, channels, KPIs). Without it, the plan has no target to optimize toward.

- The insertion order

:::q Media Strategy
question: A brand wants 10% more transactions on its site without changing the site itself. Which media objective fits best?
- Increase impressions by 10% at any click-through rate
- Increase qualified site visits by 10% at the historical conversion rate
- Reduce bounce rate by 10%
- Increase total reach by 10%
answer: 1
explain: If the site converts at a stable rate and cannot change, the lever is more qualified visits at that same rate, which scales transactions proportionally.

- Raise both reach and frequency
- Raise frequency and lower incremental reach
- Lower both reach and frequency
- Leave reach and frequency unchanged

- The second-highest bid

:::q Media Strategy
question: A brand has saturated its retargeting pool and frequency keeps climbing while new reach stalls. Which strategic shift addresses this directly?
- Raise the frequency cap so the same users see more ads
- Narrow targeting to the most frequently exposed users
- Shift budget to prospecting new audiences to expand reach
- Lower the CPM and keep buying the same pool
answer: 2

:::q Media Strategy
question: Two plans deliver the same 2,000,000 impressions. Plan A spreads them across 1,000,000 people, Plan B across 500,000 people. What is true of Plan B relative to Plan A?
- Plan B reaches more unique people
- Plan B has a lower average frequency
- Plan B and Plan A have identical frequency
- Plan B has a higher average frequency and less unique reach
answer: 3
explain: Frequency is impressions divided by reach. Plan A is 2,000,000 / 1,000,000 = 2, Plan B is 2,000,000 / 500,000 = 4. Plan B concentrates the same impressions on fewer people, so it has higher frequency and less unique reach.
:::

:::q Auctions & Pricing
question: In a classic second-price auction, four DSPs bid CPMs of 9.00, 7.20, 5.50, and 3.00 dollars. What CPM does the winner pay?
- 7.20 dollars
- 9.00 dollars
- 5.50 dollars
- 8.10 dollars
answer: 0
explain: The 9.00 dollar bid wins, but a second-price auction charges the runner-up price, so the winner pays the second-highest bid of 7.20 dollars. That gap is why truthful bidding is safe under second price.
:::

:::q Auctions & Pricing
question: A dollar reaches a publisher through two intermediaries, each keeping 15 percent of what passes through. Roughly what share of the original dollar reaches the publisher?
- About 30 cents
- About 85 cents
- About 72 cents
- About 70 cents
answer: 2
explain: Each hop leaves 0.85 of what it receives, so the publisher nets 0.85 times 0.85, which is 0.7225, about 72 cents. The take rate compounds at every hop, which is why supply path optimization shortens the chain.
:::

:::q Targeting & Identity
question: A buyer must run on Safari traffic where third-party cookies and device IDs are largely unavailable. Which approach still functions without any personal identifier?
- Cross-site frequency capping
- Contextual targeting on page content

:::q Measurement
question: A display line item records 9,000 clicks against 3,000,000 impressions. What is the click-through rate?
- 0.30 percent
- 3.0 percent
- 0.03 percent
- 30 percent
answer: 0
explain: CTR is clicks divided by impressions: 9,000 / 3,000,000 = 0.003, which is 0.30 percent. Watch the decimal, since the wrong denominator or a misplaced point is the usual error.

:::q Measurement
question: A marketer wants to know how many conversions the campaign actually caused rather than merely received credit for. Which method answers that?
- Last-touch attribution
- Data-driven attribution
- Viewable impression counting
- An incrementality test with a randomized holdout
answer: 3
explain: Only a randomized holdout compares an exposed group against a control to isolate incremental lift. Attribution methods only divide credit among conversions that already happened and cannot prove causation.
:::

:::q Inventory & Formats
question: A DOOH screen plays an ad 5,000 times in a day, and each play is seen by an estimated 28 people on average. How many DOOH impressions is that?
- 5,028
- 178
- 140,000
- 28,000
answer: 2
explain: DOOH impressions are plays times estimated audience per play: 5,000 times 28 = 140,000. Unlike a display impression mapped to one device load, a DOOH impression is a crowd estimate, so one play counts as many.
:::

:::q Inventory & Formats
question: A brand buys full-screen, unskippable CTV inventory. Which metric best reflects whether the ad held attention on that surface?
- Viewable share under the 50 percent for 1 second display rule
- Completion rate of the video
- Banner scroll depth
- Cookie match rate
answer: 1
explain: Full-screen CTV is effectively always in view, so viewability adds little. Completion rate, the share of viewers who watched the whole ad, is the metric that signals real attention for video and CTV.
:::

:::q Auctions & Pricing
question: A CPC campaign runs at a 3 dollar cost per click with a 2.5 percent click-through rate. What is the effective CPM?
- 7.50 dollars
- 75 dollars
- 0.75 dollars
- 120 dollars
answer: 1
explain: Effective CPM equals CPC times CTR times 1000: 3 times 0.025 times 1000 = 75 dollars per thousand impressions. Converting to eCPM is how a CPC deal is compared on the same scale as a flat CPM buy.
