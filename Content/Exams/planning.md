:::q Briefs & Objectives
question: A brief arrives that lists awareness, consideration, and conversion as equally important and sets no single primary objective. The problem is:
- Nothing, since covering the whole funnel is always strongest
- Without one primary objective you cannot judge tradeoffs or set a KPI
- The brief is missing a creative concept
- Three objectives is too few for a real campaign
answer: 1
explain: A brief needs one primary objective. If every stage matters equally you cannot weigh tradeoffs or pick the one number that grades the campaign, so the plan has no target to optimize toward.
:::

:::q Briefs & Objectives
question: A client briefs you to drive purchases but asks you to optimize toward impressions delivered. What is wrong?
- Nothing, impressions and purchases are the same goal
- The KPI does not match the objective, so the campaign is graded on the wrong number
- Impressions can never be measured programmatically
- Purchases belong at the awareness stage of the funnel
answer: 1
explain: Impressions are an awareness metric, while a purchase objective sits at the conversion stage and should be graded by conversion rate or cost per acquisition. Grading a conversion goal on impressions measures the wrong thing.
:::

:::q Audience & Reach
question: You hold total impressions fixed and concentrate delivery on the same users instead of spreading it. The most likely result is:
- Reach and frequency both rise
- Average frequency rises and incremental unique reach falls
- Reach and frequency both fall
- Reach and frequency are unchanged
answer: 1
explain: With a fixed impression pool, repeating exposure on the same people raises average frequency and yields less incremental unique reach. Reach has diminishing returns, so concentrated delivery buys frequency rather than new people.
:::

:::q Audience & Reach
question: A campaign delivers 45 percent reach of its target at an average frequency of 6. How many rating points is that?
- 270
- 51
- 7.5
- 270 percent
answer: 0
explain: Rating points are reach percent times frequency, so 45 times 6 equals 270. Measured against a target population these are target rating points rather than gross rating points.
:::

:::q Audience & Reach
question: Channel A reaches 6 million people in your target, channel B reaches 4 million, and their overlap is 1.5 million. What is the deduplicated reach?
- 8.5 million
- 10 million
- 11.5 million
- 5.5 million
answer: 0
explain: Inclusion-exclusion gives 6 plus 4 minus 1.5, which is 8.5 million. You subtract the overlap once because those people were counted in both channel totals, so audiences never simply add up.
:::

:::q Budget & Mix
question: Search returns 4.0 on its next dollar and display returns 1.5 on its next dollar. Under a fixed budget, what should you do?
- Move budget from display to search until their marginal returns converge
- Split the budget evenly between the two channels
- Put the entire budget into search permanently
- Move budget from search to display
answer: 0
explain: The next dollar earns more in search, so shift budget there. As search saturates and display recovers, their marginal returns move toward each other, and you stop when they meet. The optimum equalizes marginal return.
:::

:::q Budget & Mix
question: Two channels are funded at the optimal mix under a fixed budget. What is true at that point?
- The next dollar in each channel buys the same incremental outcome
- Both channels have the same average return
- All the budget sits in the higher-average channel
- The channel with the lower CPM receives everything
answer: 0
explain: Under diminishing returns the optimum equalizes marginal return across funded channels. If one channel's next dollar bought more, you would move budget there until the slopes matched. Equal average return is not the condition.
:::

:::q Budget & Mix
question: In a Hill saturation response curve, what does the parameter k represent?
- The spend level where response reaches half its maximum
- The maximum possible outcome the channel can produce
- The decay rate of carryover from earlier periods
- The cost per conversion at the optimal spend
answer: 0
explain: At spend equal to k the curve evaluates to one half, so k is the half-saturation spend, the point where you are halfway to the ceiling. Above k you are mostly paying for the flat part of the curve.
:::

:::q Measurement & Incrementality
question: A brand's typical purchase cycle runs about 60 days. The conversion attribution window should be set based on:
- The ad server's default window
- The brand's purchase cycle, so the window is long enough to capture real buyers
- The shortest window the platform offers
- The campaign's flight length alone
answer: 1
explain: Attribution windows should reflect how long real buyers take to convert. A 60-day purchase cycle needs a window long enough to capture those conversions, otherwise you undercount the ones that close late.
:::

:::q Measurement & Incrementality
question: You want a clean causal read on whether one channel actually drives sales. Which method fits best?
- A geo holdout experiment
- Multi-touch attribution
- Marketing mix modeling
- Last-touch platform reporting
answer: 0
explain: A geo holdout randomizes exposure, so the treatment-minus-control gap is true causal lift. Multi-touch attribution and marketing mix modeling are correlational and need a holdout to calibrate against.
:::

:::q Briefs & Objectives
question: A stakeholder wants the brief to chase a 2 percent click-through rate and a target cost per acquisition at the same time, with neither named as primary. Why is this a weak brief?
- It names too few metrics to grade a real campaign
- Click-through rate cannot be measured on programmatic buys
- Cost per acquisition only applies to awareness campaigns
- With no single primary metric, conflicting trade-offs cannot be decided
answer: 3
explain: A click-through goal sits at consideration and a cost per acquisition goal sits at conversion, and the two can pull the plan in opposite directions. Without one primary metric, you have no rule for choosing between them when they conflict.
:::

:::q Briefs & Objectives
question: A brief sets a pure awareness objective. Which KPI is the correct one to grade it on?
- Unique reach and cost per thousand impressions
- Conversion rate
- Cost per acquisition
- Return on ad spend
answer: 0
explain: Awareness lives at the top of the funnel, where the metrics that actually move are reach and the cost to reach, namely unique reach and cost per thousand impressions. Conversion rate, cost per acquisition, and return on ad spend are conversion-stage numbers that would grade awareness work on the wrong stage.
:::

:::q Briefs & Objectives
question: A funnel converts impressions to clicks at a 2 percent click-through rate, then clicks to sales at a 5 percent landing-page rate. The consideration stage is healthy but conversion is thin. What does the plan most need?
- More top-of-funnel reach to feed the funnel
- A higher bid on every impression
- A longer campaign flight at the same settings
- Fixing the weak conversion stage before adding reach
answer: 3
explain: Stage rates multiply, so 2 percent times 5 percent means only 0.1 percent of impressions convert, and the conversion stage is the bottleneck. Pouring more reach into a leaky bottom stage wastes money, so you repair the narrow stage first.
:::

:::q Audience & Reach
question: A total addressable market of 60 million passes three filters: 50 percent are in region, 40 percent of those are the right age, and 25 percent of those are a product fit. What is the serviceable addressable market?
- 7,500,000 people
- 3,000,000 people
- 6,000,000 people
- 12,000,000 people
answer: 1
explain: Filters compound rather than add, so SAM equals 60,000,000 times 0.50 times 0.40 times 0.25, which is 3,000,000 people. Three modest filters cut a large market down fast because each one multiplies the survivors of the last.
:::

:::q Audience & Reach
question: Three channels reach 5, 4, and 3 million in your target. The three pairwise overlaps are 1.5, 1.0, and 0.8 million, and the triple overlap is 0.3 million. What is the deduplicated reach?
- 12 million
- 7.4 million
- 9 million
- 10.5 million
answer: 2
explain: Inclusion-exclusion for three sets adds the singles, subtracts the pairwise overlaps, then adds back the triple, so (5 plus 4 plus 3) minus (1.5 plus 1.0 plus 0.8) plus 0.3 equals 9 million. The triple overlap is added back because subtracting all three pairs removed it one too many times.
:::

:::q Audience & Reach
question: A television campaign reaches 35 percent of the target population at an average frequency of 4. How many rating points is that?
- 140
- 39
- 8.75
- 140 percent
answer: 0
explain: Rating points are reach as a percent of population times frequency, so 35 times 4 equals 140. Reach is capped at 100, so the total above 100 is bought entirely with frequency, here the frequency of 4.
:::

:::q Audience & Reach
question: A digital buy delivers 4,500,000 impressions to a target population of 3,000,000 adults 25 to 54. How many target rating points is that?
- 6.75
- 1,350
- 67.5
- 150
answer: 3
explain: Target rating points are impressions delivered to the target divided by the target population, times 100, so 4,500,000 divided by 3,000,000 times 100 equals 150. That is an average frequency of 1.5 against the target, since each target person was reached 1.5 times on average.
:::

:::q Budget & Mix
question: A 10,000 dollar budget splits across two channels. Search has marginal return MR equals 12 minus spend divided by 1000, and social has MR equals 8 minus spend divided by 1000, with spend in dollars. At the equimarginal optimum the marginal returns are equal. How many dollars go to search?
- 5,000
- 7,000
- 6,000
- 8,000
answer: 1
explain: Setting 12 minus s over 1000 equal to 8 minus (10000 minus s) over 1000 gives s equals 7,000 to search and 3,000 to social. Both then return a marginal 5.0, so no dollar can be moved to improve the total.
:::

:::q Budget & Mix
question: A flight is funded with 30,000 dollars over 10 days. Using a straight-line pacing target of budget times t over T, how much should be spent by the end of day 3 to stay on pace?
- 3,000
- 10,000
- 9,000
- 30,000
answer: 2
explain: Straight-line pacing spends budget times t over T, so 30,000 times 3 over 10 equals 9,000 by the end of day 3. A pacing throttle compares actual spend to this line and tightens bidding when the campaign runs ahead so the budget lasts the whole flight.
:::

:::q Budget & Mix
question: A tax-preparation service has demand that is essentially zero most of the year and spikes hard from January through April. Which schedule fits best?
- Continuity, the same spend every week all year
- Pulsing, a steady baseline with a seasonal burst on top
- No schedule, spend whenever impressions are cheapest
- Flighting, concentrate spend in the demand window and go dark otherwise
answer: 3
explain: Demand is clustered in a short season with near-zero demand outside it, so off-season spend is wasted. Flighting bursts spend into the demand window and goes dark the rest of the year, which fits seasonal demand better than continuity or pulsing.
:::

:::q Budget & Mix
question: Adstock is a_t equals x_t plus theta times a_(t-1) with decay theta of 0.4. Last week's adstock was 60 and this week's raw spend is 120 in the same units. What is this week's adstocked value?
- 144
- 108
- 180
- 72
answer: 0
explain: Add this week's raw spend to theta times last week's adstock, so 120 plus 0.4 times 60 equals 120 plus 24 equals 144. The carried-over 24 is the tail from prior spend that still works this week.
:::

:::q Measurement & Incrementality
question: An exposed group converts at 7.2 percent and a randomized control that saw no ads converts at 5.4 percent. What is the incremental lift in percentage points?
- 7.2 percentage points
- 1.8 percentage points
- 5.4 percentage points
- 12.6 percentage points
answer: 1
explain: Incremental lift is the exposed conversion rate minus the control rate, so 7.2 minus 5.4 equals 1.8 percentage points. The control's 5.4 percent would have converted anyway, so only the 1.8-point gap was caused by the ads.
:::

:::q Measurement & Incrementality
question: A conversion campaign spends 36,000 dollars and drives 450 conversions. What is the cost per acquisition?
- 120 dollars
- 162 dollars
- 80 dollars
- 12.5 dollars
answer: 2
explain: Cost per acquisition is spend divided by conversions, so 36,000 divided by 450 equals 80 dollars. Cost per acquisition is the conversion-stage metric that grades a purchase objective, not impressions or reach.
:::

:::q Measurement & Incrementality
question: You need a broad strategic split across every channel, including offline ones with no user-level tracking. Which measurement method fits that job best?
- A geo holdout experiment on a single channel
- Last-touch platform reporting
- Marketing mix modeling
- Multi-touch attribution
answer: 2
explain: Marketing mix modeling is a top-down regression on aggregate spend and outcomes, so it covers every channel and needs no user tracking, which makes it the right tool for a strategic budget split. Geo holdouts read one channel at a time and multi-touch attribution needs user-level paths it cannot get offline.
:::
