# Start with the brief

Every campaign begins with a brief, the short document that states what the business wants and how success will be judged. A good brief names one primary objective, a budget, a timeframe, and the audience. Everything downstream, the channels you pick and the bids you set, traces back to that objective.

The classic frame for the objective is the funnel. People move from not knowing you exist, to weighing you against alternatives, to acting. Planners label these three stages awareness, consideration, and conversion. A brief that says "grow sales" is really asking you to push people down one or more of these stages.

:::callout key
A brief without a single primary objective is not a brief. If awareness, consideration, and conversion all matter equally, you cannot judge tradeoffs, and you cannot set a KPI.
:::

:::figure funnel
caption: A brief picks one primary objective, which maps to a single funnel stage: awareness, consideration, or conversion. The chosen stage decides which KPI grades the campaign.

# Match KPIs to stages

A KPI, key performance indicator, is the one number you will use to grade the campaign. The trap is grading an awareness campaign with a conversion metric. Each stage of the funnel has metrics that actually move at that stage, and forcing a downstream metric onto an upstream goal makes good work look like failure.

At awareness you measure reach and cost to reach, so cost per thousand impressions (CPM) and unique reach. At consideration you measure interest, so click-through rate, site visits, and time on site. At conversion you measure action and its cost, so conversion rate and cost per acquisition (CPA).

$$ CPA = spend / conversions $$

:::callout insight
The funnel is leaky on purpose. A 2 percent click-through rate followed by a 5 percent landing-page conversion compounds to 0.1 percent of impressions converting. Small stage rates multiply, which is why one weak stage caps the whole campaign.
:::

:::predict
prompt: A conversion campaign spends $24,000 and drives 300 conversions. What is the cost per acquisition (CPA)?
answer: 80
unit: $
hint: CPA is spend divided by conversions.
explain: CPA = $24,000 / 300 = $80. CPA is the conversion-stage metric that grades a purchase objective, not impressions or reach.
:::

# Walk the funnel

Move people through awareness, consideration, and conversion and watch how each stage rate shrinks the count reaching the bottom. Notice that improving the widest stage rarely helps if a narrow stage below it is the true bottleneck.

:::

The skill is reading where the funnel pinches. If consideration is healthy but conversion is thin, more reach wastes money. The brief tells you the goal, the funnel tells you where to spend to reach it.

:::quiz
question: A client briefs you to "drive purchases" but asks you to optimize toward impressions. What is the problem?
- The KPI does not match the objective, so the campaign will be graded on the wrong number
- Impressions are never a useful metric
- Purchases cannot be measured programmatically
- The funnel has too many stages
answer: 0
explain: Impressions are an awareness metric. A purchase objective sits at the conversion stage and should be graded by conversion rate or CPA, not by impressions delivered.
:::
